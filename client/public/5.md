The Renaissance Library in Bhiman partnered with Kamalamai Municipality Ward No. 9 to deliver a community-centered program called “Digital Resilience: Building Safe and Smart Online Presence.” The awareness drive, held at Sahid Rishiraj Devkota ‘Ajad’ Smriti Bhawan, set out to equip students, educators, and local stakeholders with practical knowledge and tools to navigate online spaces more safely.


## About the Organizers

Founded by local residents to foster reading culture and collective learning, The Renaissance Library has grown into an active community hub. The library’s ongoing activities aim to enhance educational opportunities and civic engagement across Bhiman and surrounding areas. For this initiative, the library collaborated closely with Kamalamai Municipality Ward No. 9 to reach a broader audience.

<div align="center"><img src="/blog_images/474547986_576940385237240_815585024058030306_n.jpg" width="300" height="300"></div>

## Program summary

The event was organized and led by the library’s president Shivaji Chaulagain. The session was facilitated by Gobinda Gautam, with support from library members and local social workers. The chief guest for the program was the Deputy Mayor (Upamayor) of Kamalamai Municipality. Other notable attendees included a local sub‑inspector, the Ward head of Kamalamai-09, and youth leader Binod Pradhan.

The primary audience consisted of secondary-level students (classes 9 and 10) from nearby schools including Kamala Academic School, Scholar’s Academy, Jana Jagrit Higher Secondary School, and Pragatishil Secondary School in Dandatol. Teachers, civic leaders, entrepreneurs, and concerned community members also participated. The program drew a strong turnout — more than 150 students and many community representatives — demonstrating widespread interest in digital safety.

## Keynote, learning goals and topics

<div align="center"><img src="/blog_images/475023825_576947495236529_3106081390160197500_n.jpg" width="600" height="600"></div>

Cybersecurity expert Chiranjibi Adhikari (General Secretary, CAN Federation) delivered the keynote address, sharing real-world examples of cyber incidents and practical tips to prevent and respond to online threats. Sessions emphasized:

- Understanding common cyber risks affecting youth and families
- Practical steps to protect personal data and privacy online
- Recognizing scams, phishing, and social‑engineering tactics
- Complying with legal and regulatory norms for data protection
- Digital citizenship and responsible online behaviour

Each session combined short presentations with interactive Q&A and scenario-based demonstrations so participants could immediately apply safety techniques.

## Outcomes and community impact

<div align="center"><img src="/blog_images/IMG_8311.JPG" width="600" height="600"></div>

Participants left with a clearer sense of how to identify and avoid online threats, and with concrete actions they can take to protect themselves and their communities. The Deputy Mayor praised the initiative and recognized the library’s role in bringing digital skills and awareness to young people across the district.

Organizers plan to replicate the program in other wards and schools, amplify digital‑safety outreach in partnership with local authorities, and provide follow-up resources for teachers and community volunteers.
