Last week, I walked into what I knew would be a pivotal moment in my young career. The event was the "Future Front: Educators Leadership Lab", a residential workshop with a clear objective: to equip educators with advanced leadership skills to innovate within their educational environments. On paper, it was a professional development opportunity. But for me, as a 24-year-old tuition teacher about to join a room full of seasoned principals and PhDs, it felt like so much more. It was a personal test of my own confidence, my capabilities, and my place in the world of education.

### **The Arrival: A Mix of Awe and Apprehension**

My journey began on the afternoon of June 5th, arriving at the Nyadha Hotel around 4 PM. The accommodation, graciously arranged by the Glocal Team, was impeccable and my first taste of the professional and organized approach that would define the entire event. By 5 PM, I made my way to Embark College for the orientation session. Walking into that room was like stepping into a different league.

<div align="center"><img src="/blog_images/assets/glocal_educator_img/2.jpg" width="600" height="600">Fig: All the educators at the table</div>

The group was comprised of thirteen teachers, with one female teacher among us. It was a gathering of professionals with a depth of experience that was both inspiring and incredibly daunting. There was a school principal, whose quiet authority was evident from the moment he spoke. There was a teacher with a PhD in Physics, who not only taught but also ran his own school. The room was filled with seasoned math and accounting instructors, and administrators who had traveled from all corners of Nepal—Kathmandu, Chitwan, Nuwakot, and Bhaktapur.

And then there was me. As we introduced ourselves one by one, their titles and accomplishments echoed in the room. When my turn came, the words "tuition teacher" felt small in comparison. A palpable sense of intimidation washed over me. This feeling followed us up to the college roof for coffee. While conversations began to flow more freely in the cool evening air, my own internal monologue of doubt was running loud and clear. I tried to engage, to listen, to be present, but I felt like an imposter among giants.

### **That night, sleep wouldn't come.**

After dinner at 7 PM, I returned to my hotel room, but my mind was a whirlwind. Was it the strong coffee I’d had earlier, or was it the raw, unadulterated fear of the unknown? I had never attended a training of this caliber, never been considered a peer among such senior figures. I paced the floor of my quiet room, wrestling with my anxiety, my footsteps a soft thud against the silence. I tried to coach myself, whispering, "Be confident. You have something to offer. Face what you fear." At one point, in a moment of sheer frustration, I lightly hit my head, a desperate attempt to just *stop thinking*.

But the thoughts kept coming. It wasn't until nearly 2:45 AM, feeling utterly defeated, that I sought refuge in music. I played Prem Dhoj Pradhan's timeless "Para Laijau Phool Haru," letting the melody wash over me. I leaned my back against the bed, closed my eyes, and finally, mercifully, drifted off. I slept for only three hours before my alarm buzzed, signaling the start of the main day.

### **The Day of Learning: Finding My Stride**

Running on little more than adrenaline and nervous energy, I packed my bag and headed back to the college. The main workshop day on June 6th started bright and early. After a quick breakfast, the opening and keynote sessions set a powerful, forward-thinking tone. As the day unfolded, something miraculous began to happen: my initial fear, which had felt so suffocating, began to transform into focused, energized engagement.

We participated in several workshops, but the absolute standout for me was the session on **"Developing Teamwork and Leadership Skills among Peers and Learners,"** facilitated by Mr. Joy Chowdhury.

<div align="center"><img src="/blog_images/assets/glocal_educator_img/3.JPG" width="600" height="600">Fig: Mr Joy Chowdury delivering his talks</div>

An expert in Learning and Development from India, his influence was immediate and profound. His presentation was so dynamic, his presence so engaging, and his energy so infectious that the entire room was captivated. His session was a masterclass in the **Collaborative Approach**, demonstrating how to build energy and foster genuine, productive teamwork. We all wished his 30-minute slot could have been two hours long.

<div align="center"><img src="/blog_images/assets/glocal_educator_img/4.JPG" width="600" height="600">Fig: Prof. Ujjwal Anu Chowdhury delivering his talk</div>

That session was the turning point. It unlocked something in me. Suddenly, the other workshops began to resonate on a deeper level. Prof. Ujjwal Anu Chowdhury delivered two crucial talks on **"New Age Mentoring Post Pandemic"** and **"Personal Branding of Mentors."** His words drove home the central theme of the event: our role is fundamentally shifting from the "sage on the stage" to a dynamic "mentor"—a friend, guide, and motivator. This new role demands a **Blended Learning** model, one that seamlessly integrates digital tools with face-to-face interaction to create a more personalized, supportive, and effective environment for our students.

The workshop by Anuj Man Shrestha on **"Empowering Teams Through Effective Communication"** provided the practical skills to underpin this new approach. It gave us the tools to build stronger, more meaningful connections with both our students and our peers, which is the very foundation of effective mentorship.

### **A New Beginning**

As the day concluded with a career guidance session and the final certification ceremony, I took a moment to reflect on my journey. I had walked into that hotel room feeling like an outsider, intimidated and unsure of my place. I left Embark College that evening feeling like a vital part of a forward-thinking community. The workshop armed me with not just new knowledge and pedagogical frameworks, but with a newfound confidence that had been forged in the fire of my own anxiety.

The Future Front Leadership Lab was more than just a training program; it was a profound personal and professional experience. It taught me to embrace challenges, to trust in my own potential, and to step confidently into my role as a new-age mentor. I am no longer just a tuition teacher; I am an educator, a leader, and a lifelong learner, ready to make a difference.
