## What Is This Project?

I built a website that shows Nepal's trade information in a simple way. For over 11 years (from 2071 to 2082 in Nepali calendar), Nepal has been buying and selling goods with other countries. All this information was stored in complicated Excel files that were hard to understand. My dashboard takes this messy data and turns it into colorful charts and graphs that anyone can understand.
<div align="center"><img src="/blog_images/assets/foreign_trade/trade2.png" width="700" height="700"></div>

Think of it like turning a boring math textbook into a picture book. Instead of looking at thousands of numbers in spreadsheets, you can now see beautiful charts that tell the story of Nepal's trade with the world. You can click on different parts to explore the data and find interesting patterns.

## Why Was This Needed?

Before this dashboard, if someone wanted to know about Nepal's trade, they had to do a lot of hard work. Government workers had to spend hours looking through Excel files. Business owners couldn't easily find out which countries to trade with. Students studying economics had trouble understanding real trade data. Researchers took weeks to analyze information that should take minutes.

It was like having a huge library with no organization system. All the books (data) were there, but finding what you needed was almost impossible. People were making important business and government decisions without having easy access to the information they needed.

## How Does It Work?

The dashboard is built like a smart website. When you visit it, you can choose what you want to see. Want to know which countries Nepal trades with the most? Click a button and see a colorful chart. Curious about what products Nepal exports? There's a section for that too.

I used a programming language called Python to build this. Python is great for working with data and making websites. The charts are interactive, which means when you click on them, they show you more details. It's like having a conversation with the data - you ask questions by clicking, and the charts give you answers.

The website runs on the internet, so anyone with a computer or phone can use it. No need to download anything or install special software. Just open your web browser and start exploring Nepal's trade data.

## The Technology Behind It

I built this using modern web technology, but I'll explain it in simple terms. Think of it like building a house:

**The Foundation (Data Processing):**
I used Python programming to clean up messy trade data and organize it properly. This is like sorting a huge pile of mixed-up papers into neat folders.

**The Structure (Web Framework):**
I used something called Streamlit to build the website. Streamlit is like a special tool that helps programmers quickly turn data analysis into websites that everyone can use.

**The Decoration (Charts and Graphs):**
I used Plotly to make the colorful, interactive charts. Plotly is like a digital art tool specifically designed for making data look beautiful and easy to understand.

**The Utilities (Cloud Hosting):**
The website lives on the internet through a service called Streamlit Cloud. This is like renting space on the internet where anyone can visit your website.
